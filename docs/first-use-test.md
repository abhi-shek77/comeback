# Comeback: first-use student test

**Purpose:** Find where a new student hesitates or misunderstands the prototype. This is a test of the product, not the participant.

**Time:** 10–15 minutes · **Participants:** 3–5 students · **Setup:** Open Comeback in a fresh browser profile so each participant sees onboarding.

## Opening script

“Thanks for trying this. I’m testing the prototype, not you. Please say what you’re thinking as you go. There are no right answers. I may wait a moment before helping so I can see what the screen communicates on its own.”

Avoid explaining a screen or pointing out a control before the participant has had a chance to interpret it. If they ask what to do, ask: **“What would you expect to do here?”**

## Tasks

1. **Get started:** “Imagine you want to get back into a study routine. Start wherever you think makes sense and set up Comeback for yourself.”
2. **Choose a first step:** “You’re on Today. Show me what you would do first, and complete one small task.”
3. **Try focus:** “Find a way to focus for a few minutes. Start the timer, pause it, reset it, then return to Today.”
4. **Understand progress:** “Show me where you’d look to understand how you’re progressing. What do the level and XP mean to you?”
5. **Recover after a missed day:** “Imagine you missed yesterday and feel behind. Show me what you’d do in Comeback.”

Let participants choose their own path. Don’t require them to finish the focus timer; observe whether its controls and completion reward make sense.

## Questions after the tasks

- What felt easiest to understand?
- Was anything unclear or surprising? What did you expect instead?
- Did any wording feel encouraging, pressuring, or childish?
- Which part would you use again? Which part would you ignore?
- If you could change one thing before using this tomorrow, what would it be?

## Observation notes

Record behaviors and exact phrases, not just opinions. Don’t collect names, grades, or other personal details. Ask before recording audio or video.

## Decide what to change

After 3–5 sessions, group repeated issues together. Fix blockers first, then high-severity misunderstandings. Treat a single preference as a hypothesis to check, not an automatic redesign. Keep a short list of what changed and what you want to observe in the next round.
